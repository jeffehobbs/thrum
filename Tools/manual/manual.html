Getting started

What this thing is

A drone is not a chord you play, it is a chord you inhabit. Thrum holds one indefinitely so somebody else can explore it.

Thrum has thirty-two independent voices, one per pad of the Launchpad's upper grid. A voice is not a note: it has no decay, and letting go of one is a slow fade rather than a note-off. You tap tones in, they bloom over several seconds, and they stay until you take them away.

The eight columns of the grid are the mode — seven scale degrees plus the octave — and the four rows are four octaves of it. Every pad is in the scale, so nothing you press is wrong. That is the whole point: it should be safe to build a drone without looking.

Sixty seconds to a drone

1Open Thrum. It starts in D Dorian — a Dm7 — in 5-limit just intonation, on the Harmonium timbre.
2Click Tanpura under Voicings. Four tones swell in over about seven seconds.
3Let it settle for half a minute. It should never sound quite the same twice.
4Add or remove tones from the grid. ⌥-click one to give it a sitar buzz.
5Under Pulse, press Run. The chord you are holding starts to arpeggiate through itself, two lanes at different rates. TAP four times to set the tempo by hand.
6When you're done, ⌘. lets everything go gracefully.

Why it doesn't get boring

Every partial of every voice is two oscillators offset by a fraction of a hertz. The offsets are in hertz rather than cents on purpose: a 0.3 Hz detune beats three times every ten seconds whether it sits on the fundamental or the eleventh harmonic, so the whole spectrum breathes at one slow rate instead of the top of it going ragged.

Every voice's pitch drift, tremolo, filter sweep, pan and sitar swirl run off LFOs whose periods are distinct prime numbers of seconds — 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 and up. A voice with a 41-second drift and a 67-second filter sweep returns to the same state every 2,747 seconds. Two such voices, effectively never.

One thing worth knowing. Thrum's reverb can hold a thirty-second tail. After you silence everything with ⌘. the room keeps ringing for a long time — that is correct, not a stuck note. PANIC is the one control that also clears the tail.

Temperament

A drone is the one context where tuning is fully exposed. Nothing moves, so beating between intervals is all you hear — which makes just intonation audibly, obviously different rather than a theoretical nicety.

Each pad on the grid shows how far its pitch sits from 12-TET, in cents. Changing temperament under a held drone glides.

Borrowed from ten traditions

What is worth taking from people who have been holding drones for centuries is not the scale — Thrum already has the modes — but which interval a tradition leaves out, and which register it puts the rest in.

VoicingFrom
HighlandScotlandGreat Highland pipes: a bass drone an octave under two tenors, and no fifth anywhere.
UilleannIrelandThree drones an octave apart, with the regulators' triad sitting on top.
GaidaBulgariaThe tonic under a second, in the same register — the rub village singing is built on.
AitakeJapanThe shō's gagaku cluster: fourths and seconds over two octaves, every pipe the same weight, nothing in the bass.
LauneddasSardiniaThe triple pipe — the tumbu's low drone with a fourth and a fifth laid over it.
GeorgianGeorgiaVillage polyphony over the bass: fifth, fourth and a bare seventh, and no third at all.
DidgeridooAustraliaOne fundamental and its own harmonic series, at harmonic-series levels. Almost all bottom.
HardangerNorwayThe hardingfele's sympathetic understrings, ringing quietly in one high register.
GyütöTibetGyütö chant, with the fifth down in the lowest register, where nobody else puts one.
GuqinChinaThe qin's open strings — pentatonic, so no fourth and no seventh, over all four octaves.
Two of these want other settings moved with them. Gaida is a beating interval by design, so turn Beating down or the second and the tonic fight twice over. Aitake and Hardanger deliberately hold nothing in the bottom octave — reach for Warmth if the room needs a floor.

Each is described in scale degrees, and not every mode has the flavour of fourth or fifth the original was built on — so every degree falls back, and failing that takes whichever degree simply sits in that position. Two can still land on one pad in a mode that hasn't got both: whole tone has no perfect fourth or fifth, only the tritone between them.

Pulse

A tempo, and four arpeggiators running over the drone at the same time.

An arpeggio in Thrum is not a sequence of notes. It is an accent on a voice that is already sounding: the drone keeps holding exactly as it was, and the pulse rides a fast attack-and-decay envelope on top of whichever voice it is pointing at. Tap in a chord, press Run, and the chord starts to shimmer through itself. Stop the pulse and the drone is still standing, untouched.

Because the accent is added rather than substituted, a tone that is not being held will sound for as long as its ring — so a lane can play degrees that aren't in the drone at all, over the top of the ones that are.

Transport

TAP ⌘KTap four times in time with the room. The last tap is the downbeat, and it drops every lane back onto beat zero — so re-aligning to a drummer is one gesture.
Tempo20–200 bpm. The − and + step it by one; there is a slider under Pulse in the parameter column too.
Beat dotsFour dots, one bar. The first is amber so you can find the downbeat without counting.
PresetsEight whole-rig starting points, plus all lanes off and realign lanes.
Run ⌥⌘PStarts and stops the clock, and realigns every lane when it starts. PANIC stops the clock as well as the voices.

Rates, and the eight presets

Four lanes at four rates is where the hypnosis is. Getting there is mostly a matter of not letting two of them agree.

Rates are ratios, not note values

The interesting divisions here are the ones that fight. A lane at ×1 against a lane at ×1½ takes six beats to come back around; ×1 against ×6 with a five-note set and a seven-step accent takes long enough that you stop hearing a loop at all. That drift is the whole point, and it is why the rates are written as ratios rather than as eighths and sixteenths.

Give each lane its own register and they never collide — four lanes, four octaves, four speeds. Give two lanes the same register and the same rate but different offsets and they chase each other instead.

Arpeggiate a tone with jawari armed. A plucked note through the bridge buzz is what a sitar actually is, and it is the one combination in Thrum where two features add up to a third thing.

Spatial

The drone placed around your head, and kept there when you turn it. AirPods Pro or AirPods Max.

Instead of one stereo mix, spatial mode renders sixteen mono buses positioned around you and rendered binaurally. The grid already has two axes worth putting in a room, so where a tone sits is a fact about the music rather than a decoration: the eight columns are compass points with the tonic dead ahead, and the four rows are two rings — the low two octaves slung below the ear line, the high two lifted above it.

Which means an arpeggio walking up a column climbs as well as rising in pitch, and a lane walking across the mode orbits you. Four lanes at four rates become four orbits at four speeds, which is the thing the pulse was built for.

The controls

Spatial ⌥⌘SIn or out. Everything else in the app behaves identically either way.
Head ⌥⌘HReads your AirPods' orientation and counter-rotates the listener, so the drone stays where it is in the room as you turn. Asks for Motion permission the first time; declining leaves the field head-locked and nothing else changes.
Recenter ⌥⌘RTakes wherever you are looking now as straight ahead. AirPods yaw drifts, so this is the button you will actually use — and the one to hit if the field ever feels tilted.
RadarTop-down view. Two rings of eight, inner is the low octaves; each dot lights with what that bus is putting out. The line from the centre is where your head is pointing, and the tick at the top is straight ahead.
Field Radius · LiftUnder Space in the parameter column. How far out the rings sit, and how far apart the two octave tiers are pushed vertically.
Two things to get right or it will not sound like much. Set macOS's own Spatial Audio to Off for your AirPods — two lots of HRTF smear each other. And turn Wet down: the reverb tail deliberately bypasses the spatial stage, because a thirty-second diffuse tail has no location to be placed at, so it stays a stereo bed and does not rotate with your head. The more of it you have, the less the field localizes.

What it costs

Almost nothing. The sixteen HRTF instances add about 0.2% of one core on top of the engine, measured with the app's own graph. Spatial mode is not the thing to switch off if you are chasing a dropout.

Jawari

The sitar control, in detail

A sitar's bridge is flat and wide, so the string grazes it as it vibrates and sheds a cascade of upper harmonics locked to the fundamental. That grazing is called jawari, and it is the reason a sitar shimmers.

It is per-tone, and it is armed rather than dialled

Sitar Depth is the value written to a tone when you arm it. With nothing armed, moving the slider does nothing audible. Moving it while tones are armed updates all of them together.

In the app⌥-click a pad.
On the LaunchpadHold SITAR and tap tones. Pressing into the SITAR pad sets the depth live, from 15% to 100%.
By timbreTanpura, Bagpipe, Shruti Box and Harmonium carry a floor that applies to every voice with nothing armed.

What the depth actually drives

Three things move together as you raise it:

At 0 → 100%
Phaser mix
0 → 75%		Four cascaded allpass stages whose coefficient is swept by an LFO on a prime-derived period, unique per voice — so no two sitar tones ever swirl together. This is the "slight phase" part.
Comb feedback
0.55 → 0.83		A comb exactly one period of the fundamental long, so it reinforces the harmonic series rather than fighting it. This is the sympathetic ring.
Buzz mix
0 → 30%		High-passed at 700 Hz, so only the bright edge is added and none of the mud.

Changes glide in over a second or two rather than switching.

Use it on one voice, not all of them. The effect is per-tone by design. Arm a single high tone and leave the rest clean and you get the tanpura's shimmering top string over a steady bed. Arm everything and it smears.

Two honest limitations

The waveshaper is an odd function, so it produces odd harmonics only — 3rd, 5th, 7th. A real jawari gives you the whole series, so this reads a little hollow beside the real thing.

There is a hard clip inside the comb's feedback loop, needed because the shaper grows with level rather than compressing. Hard clipping produces harmonics above Nyquist that fold back as aliasing, so at high depth on the upper rows you may hear a gritty metallic edge that isn't musical buzz. If it bothers you, keep the depth moderate on the top octave.

Rows 8–5 · the tones

Thirty-two pads: eight scale degrees across, four octaves up. Row 5 is the lowest, row 8 the highest. Chord tones glow amber when idle, the rest of the scale sits nearly dark.

How it plays

Rows 8–5One row per lane, one pad per rate. Pressing a rate arms the lane as well as setting it; pressing the rate already lit turns the lane off. Keep a finger here — retuning the polyrhythm live is the most useful gesture on the page.
Rows 4 · 3Lane N is always column N, so the lanes read as four vertical stripes in the app's own four colours. Each pad cycles that lane's pattern, register, offset or source forward by one.
Row 2TAP · RUN · all lanes off · realign · halve · double · swing · unsteady. RUN breathes on the beat while the clock is going.
Row 1The eight presets. One press replaces all four lanes and the tempo.
Top rowTempo by ±1 and ±5, and TAP sitting right beside BANK so one hand holds while the other taps. PANIC stays PANIC — the one key that means the same thing on every layer.

Launch Control · notes

Launch Control

Plug one in and its knobs land on Thrum's parameters in the order the app shows them. Nothing to configure.

Both modelsThe Launch Control (16 knobs) and the Launch Control XL (24 knobs + 8 faders) are told apart automatically and get different factory-template CC maps.
Soft takeoverA knob does nothing until it passes through the parameter's current value, so grabbing one never jumps the sound mid-performance.
MIDI learnClick a parameter's name in the app, then move a knob. The binding is saved and survives relaunching.
PadsTrigger the first six voicings, plus let-go and panic. Capped at six on purpose: there are fifteen voicings now, and letting them fill all eight pads would cost the panic button.

Both devices report themselves as "Launch Control"; only the XL appends "XL", and that is what Thrum keys off. If your device is on a non-factory template, learn the knobs once and it will remember.

Troubleshooting

If something sounds wrong

Crackle, ticks, dropoutsCheck the DSP readout in the top bar. If it shows drops in red, the engine is missing its deadline. Nothing sounding? Then it isn't Thrum. Never run a Debug build — the engine is roughly 40× slower without optimisation and cannot keep up.
Sound continues after silencingThat's the reverb tail, which can run thirty seconds. PANIC clears it; Let go and Cut short deliberately don't.
Grid is dark or unresponsiveCheck the device pill in the top bar. Unplug and replug — Thrum rescans on any MIDI change and re-enters programmer mode.
Launchpad in the wrong modeThrum uses the "LPX MIDI" port, not "LPX DAW". If another app has claimed the device or left it in a different mode, quit that app and replug.
Sitar Depth does nothingExpected on timbres with no floor. Arm a tone first: ⌥-click, or hold SITAR and tap.
A tone won't tap offYou're pressing too hard or too long and it reads as a level ride. Hold LET GO and tap it instead.
Pulse is running but silentEvery lane defaults to the Held source, so it needs tones held to arpeggiate. Either tap a chord in, or switch a lane to Chord or Scale, which draw from the mode whether or not anything is sounding.
Arpeggio sounds mechanicalRaise Unsteady, and give two lanes rates that don't divide into each other — ×1 with ×1½ rather than ×1 with ×2. Equal rates lock; unequal ones drift.
Arpeggio buries the droneLower Accent, or lengthen Ring so the notes overlap into the chord instead of standing out of it.
Spatial sounds like ordinary stereoAlmost always macOS's own Spatial Audio also being on for the AirPods — set it to Off. Failing that, Wet is high enough that the un-spatialized reverb bed is drowning the field; bring it down.
The field turns the wrong wayHit Recenter facing forward. If a tone still follows your head instead of staying put, that is a genuine bug rather than a setting — the sign convention is measured in Tools/axis.
Head tracking never startsOnly AirPods Pro and AirPods Max report orientation. If it was denied, Thrum appears under Privacy & Security → Motion & Fitness.
Everything sounds stillBeating, Drift or Motion is at zero. Those three are what keep it alive.
Tiring after a whileRaise Presence Cut. Energy around 2–5 kHz is what wears the ear out over twenty minutes.
A drone wants patience. Most of what Thrum does happens on cycles of forty seconds to a minute and a half. If you audition it for ten seconds at a time you will hear a pad; give it five minutes and you will hear the instrument.
